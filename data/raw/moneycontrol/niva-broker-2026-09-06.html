India, China to hold first Corps Commander-level talks in Arunachal Pradesh

The armies of India and China are set to hold their first Corps Commander-level talks in Arunachal Pradesh on Sunday, as the two sides continue efforts to address issues along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) in the eastern sector.

The meeting is scheduled at the Wacha-Damai border meeting point on the Indian side and is expected to focus on unresolved issues in the area, sources said. The Indian side will be represented by Lt Gen Girish Kalia, Commander of the 3 Corps.

The Indian Army has two Corps formations responsible for the LAC in Arunachal Pradesh, the Tezpur-based 4 Corps and the Rangapahar-based 3 Corps. Sources said the Indian Army has adopted a firm position regarding the presence and activities of Chinese troops in areas where differences persist. India and China have differing perceptions of the LAC, which have periodically led to friction along the border.

First Corps Commander talks in Arunachal Pradesh

The Wacha meeting marks the first time Corps Commander-level talks between the two armies are being held in Arunachal Pradesh. The two sides have previously conducted such military-level discussions at the Chusul-Moldo border meeting point in the Ladakh sector.

Those talks gained prominence following the 2020 Galwan Valley clashes, which resulted in casualties on both sides. The latest engagement follows National Security Adviser Ajit Doval’s visit to China, after which New Delhi and Beijing announced several measures aimed at maintaining peace and tranquillity along the LAC.

Doval held talks with Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi in Beijing under the Special Representatives mechanism, with discussions covering the boundary question and trans-boundary cooperation, PTI reported.

China remains a long-term security challenge

Former Chief of Defence Staff General Anil Chauhan (retd) welcomed reports of greater military communication between India and China, including the possibility of hotlines and Corps Commander-level talks in the Northeast.

Speaking at a lecture on ‘Threats and Challenges to India’s Security’ at Teen Murti Bhawan on Saturday, Chauhan described the developments as “a welcome development".

He said China represents an “absolutely different and more long-term and comprehensive challenge". “The boundary issue remains unresolved, and there’s no mutually agreed definition even on the Line of Actual Control. Differences in perception, combined with infrastructure development and forward military presence, create the possibility of friction and escalation," he said, according to PTI.

Chauhan stressed that maintaining stability along the border requires “vigilance, credible capabilities, clear agreements and consistent adherence to them".

He also said India-China ties should not be viewed solely through the boundary dispute, noting that both countries are major Asian powers and large economies that cooperate and compete while holding different views on regional and global affairs. “The relationship between the two countries cannot be held hostage to only boundary issues," Gen Chauhan said.
